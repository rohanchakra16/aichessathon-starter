- Title: Halfmove-clock progress gradient in the leaf evaluation for reduced-material endgames
- Family: leaf-evaluation-progress-gradient
- Hypothesis: In agent.py's _model_evaluate, fold a cheap side-relative non-king material total into the existing piece loop (accumulate material_diff += sign * SEE_VALUES[piece_type] * len(squares) for piece_type != chess.KING, reusing the squares list and sign already computed). After the midgame/endgame blend and the existing mobility/castling terms, apply a bounded progress gradient gated to reduced-material, materially decided endgames: if phase <= 10 (both sides reduced to roughly a queen, or a rook plus a minor, or less) AND abs(material_diff) >= 300 (at least a clean minor-piece advantage for one side), compute pull = min(board.halfmove_clock, 80) * 0.35 and do score -= math.copysign(pull, material_diff). This penalises the side that is ahead for a rising 50-move counter (maximum about 28 model units, below a pawn and far below any mate score, so it can only break ties and can never invert a position material already calls won or lost), giving alpha-beta a monotone reason to prefer clock-resetting pawn moves, captures and trades over the king/piece shuffling that currently produces 32 of 33 draws by threefold repetition; and symmetrically it rewards the side that is behind for a rising counter, giving the engine a reason to steer lost endgames toward the 50-move draw rather than into the long king hunts that account for all 18 decisive losses. No change to _ordered_moves, _quiescence, _negamax, the transposition-table key or size, _forced_draw, _budget_seconds, the iterative-deepening loop, MATE handling, or weights/.
- Rationale: The dominant retained failure across six straight experiments is non-forced threefold-repetition draws, many from winning or equal positions, plus a loss column that is entirely checkmates after passive simplification. The mechanical cause is specific and untouched: the trained piece-square model has no move-counter feature, its PST is identical for a king or piece shuffled between two squares, and the accepted mobility term (exp-0089) is near zero for a lone or cramped king, so the leaf evaluation cannot distinguish steady progress from aimless shuffling in a technically winning position, and a missing gradient cannot be recovered by searching deeper because the converting mate is 15-25 plies past the horizon. A halfmove-clock term is the minimal closed-form way to inject that gradient. It is materially different from every exhausted mechanism: draw contempt (exp-0063) only re-values an already-recognised draw and supplies no direction toward the win; in-tree repetition scoring (exp-0094) changes a search-tree value and needs the repetition already on the board or in the line; mate-distance scoring acts only once a mate is found in-tree; and exp-0095's coordinate mop-up term was gated to bare-king endings that fired in essentially none of the 64 arena games, so it left endgame-conversion evaluation genuinely untested rather than refuted. A clock gradient fires in every reduced-material endgame regardless of piece configuration (queen, rook, rook-versus-minor, king-and-pawn races) and directly rewards the concrete progress moves the engine currently declines. It keeps the trained model fully determinative of every middlegame and every non-reduced endgame, and of every model-move-ablation opening, and it is a roughly ten-line self-contained change with four hand-chosen scalars, no coefficient from training and no weights change.
